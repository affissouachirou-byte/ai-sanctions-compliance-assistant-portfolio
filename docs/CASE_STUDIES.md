# Two synthetic investigation case studies

All names, payments and identification data are fictional. These examples illustrate **workflow safeguards**, not positive sanctions decisions.

## Case A — `SYN-ADV-0332-001`

**Context:** fictional EUR 125,000 payment with a Swiss-bank context and a German beneficiary.

**Illustrative ownership inputs:** 60% × 40% + 10% = **34% economic interest**. Separate voting inputs produce 70% × 45% + 5% = **36.5% voting interest**.

**What matters:** these calculations alone do not answer whether the entity is owned/controlled for OFAC, EU or Swiss sanctions purposes. The workflow retains unresolved designation, legal control, applicable restriction, payment nexus and evidence questions.

**AI-assurance result:** two original AI legal propositions quoted traceable passages but attributed duties those passages did not establish. The claims were reviewed as unsupported. The corrected historical-workflow report preserves claim-level assessments without placing the propositions under verified current-law findings.

**Do not claim:** a definitive blocking outcome, validated current-law advice, or a verified ownership/control determination.

## Case B — `SYN-EVAL-ID-001`

**Context:** fictional CHF 18,400 payment and two possible identity candidates.

| Identity evidence | Candidate A | Candidate B |
|---|---|---|
| Name similarity | 100/100 | 100/100 |
| Date-of-birth assessment | `CONTRADICTS` | `MISSING` |

A separate upstream screening score is **94/100**; it should not be conflated with the candidate-name scores.

**What matters:** a conflicting DOB and an unavailable DOB represent different evidence states. Neither candidate was treated as conclusively identified or designated. Human review remained required.

**Do not claim:** that 100/100 name similarity proves a true sanctions match.
